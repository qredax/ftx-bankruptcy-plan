# 7.4 — Record dates and delivery

## The window

{% hint style="info" %}
Page 63 of the plan.
{% endhint %}

The record date for a distribution is set no less than 45 and no more than 75 days before the distribution date itself.

Whoever appears on the register on that date is paid in that round. Missing it moves a holder to the next cycle. It does not reduce or cancel the claim.

This is the deadline most coverage focuses on, and it is the least dangerous of the three in the plan.

## Transfers close to a record date

> If a Claim is transferred 45 or fewer days before the applicable Distribution Record Date, the Distribution Agent shall make distributions to the transferee only to the extent practical, and, in any event, only if the relevant transfer form contains an unconditional and explicit certification and waiver of any objection to the transfer by the transferor.

Two things follow.

The first is timing. Between signing and appearing on the register there is the transfer notice itself, then a twenty-one day objection period under Rule 3001(e). Against a 45-day cut-off that is not a comfortable margin.

The second is drafting. The certification and waiver language should be in every transfer form rather than only in transfers made close to a record date. It costs nothing to include and decides the outcome when it matters.

## Withholding

{% hint style="info" %}
Pages 63–64.
{% endhint %}

> The Plan Administrator may, but shall not be required to, withhold any Distribution to a Holder of an Allowed Claim upon receipt of written notice to the Plan Administrator of pending litigation concerning an executed agreement by such Holder to sell such Allowed Claim in order to seek specific instructions of the Bankruptcy Court prior to making such Distribution.

A written notice is enough. No court order is required to trigger it, and the administrator and related parties carry no liability either way.

The practical consequence is that a seller can freeze a payout on a claim already sold, with a single letter.

## What this means for a transfer

Three things worth putting in writing between the parties.

The certification and waiver language, in every transfer form.

An obligation on the seller not to send the administrator notice of a dispute over the sale agreement, with a pre-litigation procedure that has to be exhausted first.

Enough lead time before a record date to absorb the notice and the objection period.
