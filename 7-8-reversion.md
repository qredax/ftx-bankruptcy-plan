# 7.8 — Unclaimed distributions

{% hint style="info" %}
Pages 65–66 of the plan. Section heading: Reversion.
{% endhint %}

## What triggers it

The six months do not run from a calendar date. A distribution has to have been assigned and then left unclaimed. Until a payment exists, there is nothing for the period to run against.

That distinction matters for anyone whose claim is still disputed, because no distribution is created while it stays that way.

## What happens at the end

> Upon revesting pursuant to this Section 7.8, the Claim of any Holder or its successors and assigns with respect to such property shall be cancelled and forever barred, notwithstanding any applicable federal or state escheat, abandoned or unclaimed property laws to the contrary.

Two things in that sentence are worth reading twice.

**"Successors and assigns."** A purchaser inherits the loss. If a distribution was forfeited before a claim changed hands, the new holder cannot recover it.

**"Notwithstanding any applicable escheat law."** The usual route for recovering property that has passed to a state does not apply. It is closed off in the text.

## What the Trust owes in return

This is the part that has not appeared in coverage.

> provided, however, that the Plan Administrator and the Distribution Agent shall use commercially reasonable efforts to notify the Holder of such Unclaimed Distribution within three months of the applicable Distribution Date. The Plan Administrator and the Distribution Agent are deemed to have satisfied their use of commercially reasonable efforts by mailing a notice to the last known address of record known to the Claims Agent and emailing such notice to the last known email address of record known to the Claims Agent.

A warning is supposed to arrive halfway through the six months, by post and by email.

For anyone whose payment is stuck, that notice is the first thing to look for. Everything written about the forfeiture leaves out that the obligation runs in both directions.

Note also how the obligation is discharged: by mailing and emailing to the addresses on file. Read alongside §7.14, which treats a request sent to the last known address as received, the notice can be satisfied without ever reaching anyone.

## After the final distribution

> If any Unclaimed Distribution revests in any Wind Down Entity as Plan Assets pursuant to this Section 7.8 after the Final Distribution is made, the Plan Administrator shall not be required to make any subsequent Distributions to Holders of Allowed Claims or Interests under the Plan.

Late in the case, a reversion is not a delay. There is no subsequent round to catch.
