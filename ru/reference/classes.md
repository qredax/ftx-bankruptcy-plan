# Claim classes

{% hint style="warning" %}
Percentages as at 31 July 2026 and stated by class. A figure quoted without its class is wrong for half of the people reading it.
{% endhint %}

| Class | Description                                    | Cumulative |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| 5A    | Dotcom customer entitlement claims             | 105%       |
| 5B    | US customer entitlement claims                 | 105%       |
| 6A    | General unsecured claims                       | 103%       |
| 6B    | Digital asset loan claims                      | 103%       |
| 7     | Convenience claims, allowed at $50,000 or less | 120%       |

The Trust's own announcement gives 120% for Class 7. The customer portal has shown 120.5%, and the Trust noted that final percentages may shift slightly on rounding.

## What the percentage is of

Of the allowed claim amount, which is fixed at the dollar value of the assets on the petition date of 11 November 2022.

It is not a percentage of what those assets are worth now. Whatever was on the exchange became a dollar figure that day and has stayed one, and the Trust pays dollars.

This is why a recovery above 100% and a sense of having lost money are both accurate at the same time. Someone who held bitcoin receives the November 2022 dollar figure plus interest, not the coin.

## Increments in the fifth round

The cumulative figures above came from these increments on 31 July 2026: 9% for 5A, 5% for 5B, 3% each for 6A and 6B.

Different classes move at different rates, so two claims can sit at the same cumulative percentage and have arrived there by different routes.

## Class 7

The convenience class sits at a fixed cumulative percentage and does not receive further instalments the way the other classes do.

Which means a dispute on a convenience class claim holds the whole amount rather than an increment.

## Why recovery exceeds 100%

Where an estate recovers more than it owes, unsecured creditors may receive post-petition interest on top of the face value of their claims.

The FTX estate recovered materially more in assets than the total of allowed claims, which is what produced figures above par. It is unusual in a bankruptcy and it is not a windfall: the base it is calculated on is the November 2022 valuation.
