# 7.10 — Setoffs

{% hint style="info" %}
Page 66 of the plan.
{% endhint %}

## The estate's side

The debtors may set off their own claims against an allowed claim, and against distributions on it, before a distribution is made. No separate notice is required and no court approval is needed.

## The holder's side

A holder may set off a counterclaim against the estate only if that claim was asserted in the case before the applicable bar date, and only where there is a final court determination.

## Why the asymmetry matters

Anyone who withdrew funds from the exchange in the ninety days before the petition date has potential preference exposure. If the estate has a claim on that basis, it can be applied against a distribution without a hearing.

That turns preference review from a theoretical question into a practical one, including for anyone acquiring a claim: the exposure sits on the claim, not on the person who created it.

## What this means for a transfer

Worth covering in writing between the parties.

A representation from the seller that there are no counterclaims from the estate, including preference actions.

Disclosure of withdrawals in the ninety days before the petition date.

This sits alongside the disqualification question under §502(d), which in the Third Circuit travels with the claim rather than staying with the transferor.
